This study asks whether large language models reason over tax statute or reproduce answers seen during training. It compares models that answer directly in natural language with a neuro-symbolic pipeline, in which the model translates a case into Prolog and a solver carries out the inference.

## How it works

The work builds on SARA, the StAtutory Reasoning Assessment dataset. SARA pairs natural-language statutes and cases with human-coded Prolog, for deciding entailment and for computing tax owed.

In the neuro-symbolic pipeline, the LLM converts a case's fact pattern into Prolog facts. Those facts join SARA's statute knowledge base along with a query such as `owes_tax(alice, 2015, X)`, and the solver binds `X` to 14,000.

Two additions separate reasoning from memorization. Contamination quizzes check whether a model can pick the verbatim SARA instance out of meaning-preserving perturbations. SARA+ adds new splits that change the numbers in the rules and cases and paraphrase the text, so each problem keeps its structure while its answer changes.

## Why it's interesting

When both the rules and the cases are perturbed, GPT-5's direct answers on numerical entailment fall from 98% to 73%, while the Prolog pipeline with the same model stays at 95%. This traces much of the direct models' earlier high scores to benchmark exposure, and it shows that statutory tax reasoning favors neuro-symbolic methods over standalone LLMs.
